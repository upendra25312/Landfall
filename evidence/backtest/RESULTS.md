**Spread 5.7%** (within ±15%). Variance analysis:
- Highest **engine** ($16,451), lowest **bands** ($15,548); the difference is entirely in the VM compute line (70% of the total — storage + run-rate extras are shared and damp the spread).
- The **engine** sits at or above the other two: it prices the exact right-sized SKU, so memory-heavy VMs land on the constrained-vCPU E-family at its ~1.28x premium, which the linear rate and the D/F-based bands only partly capture.
- **blended** ($/vCPU x right-sized vCPU) tracks within a few percent because the estate's right-sized footprint is dominated by the D/F families the rate is median-derived from; it drifts low on the E-family boxes.
- **bands** rounds each VM to a representative SKU priced as the D/F mean at the band ceiling — close to the engine for D/F workloads, a little low for the memory-optimised tail (1.15x surcharge vs the engine's full E-family price).
- Only 50% of servers carry utilisation data, so the rest are not right-sized down — this lifts all three methods equally (no effect on the spread, but the absolute number is soft and confidence is Low).

## Reading this

The three methods share the right-sized target footprint, the storage cost and the run-rate extras; only the **VM compute pricing** differs — catalogue SKU, linear $/vCPU, and T-shirt bands. Agreement within ±15% means the headline run-rate is a property of the estate, not of the SKU catalogue or the reservation model. It is **not** a claim of real-world price accuracy — that is the province of the live Pricing-Calculator POE (E11.16) and the quarterly price re-benchmark (S2).
